Finite Sturm search bounds

For descending coefficients a[0] through a[n], Fujiwara's bound is
2*max(abs(a[i]/a[0])**(1/i)), for i=1..n. With frexp(a[i])=m[i]*2**e[i]
and abs(m[i]) in [0.5,1), abs(a[i]/a[0]) is strictly less than
2**(e[i]-e[0]+1). The solver therefore brackets roots below the finite power
of two 2**K, where K=1+max(ceil((e[i]-e[0]+1)/i)). No coefficient ratio is
formed to compute this bound. Unsupported bound exponents produce an error.

Ordinary monic normalization retains the original variable. A nonzero term
that underflows to zero during that normalization produces an error. If a
division would overflow, the solver instead uses x=2**K*y
and constructs normalized coefficients using frexp/scalbn. This fallback checks
that nonzero terms remain representable and above the existing SMALL_ENOUGH
coefficient threshold. It rejects incompatible coefficient ranges explicitly.
It must not silently remove a small-world root from a mixed-scale polynomial.

The Sturm chain is checked for nonfinite coefficients during construction and
normalization. Sign evaluation falls back to mantissa/exponent Horner arithmetic
if ordinary evaluation overflows or returns zero. Zero values use the first
nonzero derivative to obtain the left-limit sign. This gives consistent root
counts on half-open intervals [a,b), including repeated roots at bisection
points. The bracket [0,2**K) retains the public solver's historical zero-root
result. A zero upper endpoint cannot replace the different root inside that
interval during false-position refinement.

False-position interpolation stays inside the bracket and falls back to Sturm
bisection when its values are unsupported. Bisection uses an overflow-safe
midpoint and a budget derived from the binary64 exponent span.

Run actual Solve_Polynomial CPU controls with a configured matching build:

  sh tools/bench/check-polynomial-distance.sh BUILD_DIRECTORY OUTPUT_DIRECTORY

Controls cover all Sturm orders 3..35, distinct/repeated/zero roots, no positive
roots, uniform coefficient scaling, mixed near and distant roots, lunar and
solar distances, monic normalization overflow, overflowing polynomial values,
and explicit rejection of unsupported coefficient/chain/bound ranges. Repeated
roots use a multiplicity-aware 8*sqrt(machine epsilon) relative error gate;
the other known-root controls use 2e-9 relative error.

This does not promise arbitrary conditioning or every representable root range.
The public solver still discards leading coefficients below 1e-10 and retains
its existing coefficient-zero and convergence tolerances. Incompatible scaled
coefficients, overflowing Sturm chains and unrepresentable strict bounds report
errors. Finite inputs alone do not guarantee that the binary64 Sturm chain is
numerically supported.
