3.12 Volume Lights

A volume of medium can be declared a light. It lights the surfaces and scattering media around it, casts shadows shaped by its own volume, and states its own colour and brightness in a light block beside the media, separate from the glow the camera sees. A candle flame, a neon tube, a fireball, a glowing jellyfish and a lantern inside a cloud are all one interior with a light block.

This chapter describes the block, the radiance law that gives the light its power and falloff, how it is sampled, how it meets light groups, radiosity and other media, and its limits. The media themselves are described in Emission and Density Drives the Coefficients. The sample scenes light_*.pov and blend_candle.pov in scenes/interior/media are the worked examples. The examples in this chapter assume #version 4.0;.

3.12.1 Making a Volume a Light

A light statement inside an interior statement makes the object an independent volume light. The word light is matched by its text before a block, so it stays free as an identifier everywhere else. The block belongs to the interior, not to any one medium: one interior, one light, using the object's shape and its own pigment or the combined emission of its own media. An interior declared as an identifier, or copied, keeps its light.

INTERIOR_LIGHT:
  light { [INTERIOR_LIGHT_ITEM...] }
INTERIOR_LIGHT_ITEM:
  pigment { PIGMENT } | emission | brightness Value [nits | lumens] |
  attenuation Value [, Value] | samples Integer |
  shadowless [Bool] | media_interaction [Bool] | media_attenuation [Bool] |

Interior light default values:

pigment          : solid white
emission         : absent (use pigment)
brightness       : 1.0 nits
attenuation      : 2, 1 (physical)
samples          : 16
shadowless       : off
media_interaction: on
media_attenuation: on
pigmentThe light's own colour or pattern, evaluated in the container's coordinates. The pattern distributes emitted light independently of media density. Default solid white.
emissionA bare flag selecting the full combined RGB emission of this interior's own media, including density patterns and media mixing. It takes no value and cannot be combined with pigment. Absorption, scattering and overlapping objects do not supply source colour. Brightness and its units retain their normal meaning.
brightnessA non-negative source brightness. A unitless value and nits use the topology-normalized radiance convention below; lumens specifies total generated luminous flux under the convention 1 scene unit = 1 m. See Radiance and Attenuation.
attenuationThe falloff: a power and a scale, 2, 1 by default, which is physical. A scale of 0 turns the falloff off. See Radiance and Attenuation.
samplesAn integer of at least 1: emitter draws per lit point, and so the shadow rays. More samples give smoother soft shadows and cost more. See Sampling and Shadows.
shadowlessThe light casts no shadows, as a shadowless light does.
media_interactionWith off, the light does not light scattering media, as for a normal light source.
media_attenuationWith off, a shadow ray is not dimmed by the media it passes; see Atmospheric Attenuation.
photonsPhoton options for the light: reflection and refraction switches, as a light source's photons block. The scene photon configuration and collecting targets remain necessary; these switches select reflected and refracted photon routes.

The block takes no position. The light is placed, transformed and copied with its object. The container must be finite. This example authors a separate visible glow; matching its colour does not make its angular radiance equal to the cast light:

sphere {
  0, 1 pigment { rgbt 1 }
  interior {
    media { emission rgb <14, 6, 1.6> * 9 density { spherical color_map { [0 rgb 0] [0.4 rgb 0.3] [1 rgb 1] } } }
    light { pigment { rgb <14, 6, 1.6> * 9 } samples 32 }
  }
  scale <0.06, 0.16, 0.06> translate <0, 1.45, 0>
}

3.12.1.1 Using Media Emission as the Source

The bare emission flag reuses the media emission field without repeating its colour or density pattern. It is available at every scene version and in .pov4. Without it, the light still defaults to solid white. For example:

interior {
  media { emission rgb <100, 40, 10> density { spherical } }
  light { emission brightness 12 lumens }
}

This generates 12 lumens using the combined RGB emission field as its distribution. The media's visible glow is unchanged. A nonzero lumen setting requires a positive source integral; absent or zero emission cannot normalize it. See scenes/interior/media/light_emission.pov.

3.12.2 Radiance and Attenuation

The light uses its declared emitting region and source field. By default that field is its own pigment, independent of media density. With light { emission }, it is instead the full combined emission RGB of the same interior's media, after density and media mixing. Absorption, scattering, refraction and other objects do not contribute to this source field. Let V be the region's geometric volume, A its true boundary area and C(x) the selected source field. Unitless brightness and nits use reference length ℓ = 4V/A and isotropic emission coefficient j(x) = L C(x)/ℓ. Exposed cavity walls contribute to A; internal union seams do not. This is not a convex hull or projected silhouette normalization. The declared emitting region affects this reference calibration: padding its container while keeping a smaller pigment field unchanged is not guaranteed to preserve source strength.

A uniform white source generates flux πLA. This is a predictable volume-source reference, not identical radiance along every chord or identical illumination at every nearby point. With no absorbing or scattering media and a white Lambertian probe of unit reflectance, a uniform unit cube gives about 0.9257L at its centre with an axis-aligned normal and 1.2399L immediately outside a face centre facing inward. A sphere gives 0.75L at its centre and L immediately outside facing inward. Geometry, receiver orientation and pigment determine actual illumination; there is no cell-sized near-distance clamp.

lumens specifies generated flux Φ, before absorption on the way out. It normalizes j(x) = ΦC(x)/(4π∫Y(C(x)) dV), where Y is the renderer's existing greyscale convention (RGB weights 0.297, 0.589 and 0.114, with white equal to one). A pattern redistributes the stated total; uniformly multiplying a nonzero pigment does not change that total. This is the renderer's established luminance convention, not a new spectral or colour-management model. A sampled zero integral cannot normalize nonzero stated lumens; a narrow source missed by preparation may also produce this diagnostic.

The volume emitter is the reference model. An authored surface emitter can be an approximation with somewhat different angular and near-field illumination; exact surface/volume identity is not promised. A transparent emitting volume is also not automatically an opaque monitor or a frosted lamp envelope. Authors choose source geometry, backing and any separate scattering diffuser to represent those objects; no geometric angular multiplier silently turns a transparent source into a diffuse surface.

For nits, uniformly scaling geometry and receiver positions by s divides j by s, multiplies generated flux by s2, and preserves illumination. At fixed lumens, j instead scales by 1/s3 and illumination by 1/s2. To preserve the optical appearance of coexisting media, their inverse-length absorption and scattering coefficients must also scale by 1/s; resizing the same physical material is a different operation. No light unit reads mm_per_unit. Lumens assume one scene unit is one metre; scale_lumens(Phi, c) returns Phi / c2 for author-stated metres per scene unit c.

Physical divergence follows the finite-source integral and approaches inverse-square behavior at large distance. attenuation Power, Scale adds an artistic, nonconservative path modifier. Write p for Power, a for Scale, R for the bounding radius, d for source-to-receiver path length and q = R/max(d,R). For positive a the modifier is qp−2aqp−1. The default 2, 1 is exactly one. At distances within R it stays one; with power 2 its distant limit is 1/a. A zero scale compensates distant inverse-square divergence. One value retains the scale-only shorthand, with power 2. The same modifier applies to direct and photon paths. Classic fade_power and fade_distance are not accepted in this block.

The medium's authored emission is appearance alone: camera, reflection and refraction rays see it, but it creates no radiosity or photon energy. Light genuinely scattered by the medium remains eligible for indirect transport. A lantern's visible glow comes from its haze scattering the light, or from an authored emission look. Use light { emission } to reuse the glow's RGB field as the light input. This does not equate camera glow and cast radiance: default brightness is still 1 nit with the reference-length calibration above.

3.12.3 Sampling and Shadows

Preparation estimates ℓ from isotropic lines through the emitting region: occupied path length divided by the count of uninterrupted occupied intervals. Full intersection lists include cavity walls and merge internal union seams. Four independently scrambled estimates use 1024, 4096 and at most 16384 lines each, stopping when their maximum relative discrepancy is at most 0.5%. A discrepancy warning reports failure to meet that target at the limit. This is an empirical replicate check, not a certified error bound; thin geometry and narrow procedural pigment peaks can evade every preparation probe.

Preparation publishes an immutable RGB light field on the interior's shared world-space layout before tracing. Solid pigment colours remain analytic; other source fields are sampled on that layout and reconstructed by trilinear interpolation. The source geometry clips this field when light is evaluated, without masking grid nodes at curved boundaries. Runtime sampling never evaluates the authored source pattern. This sampled field approximates the authored pattern: subcell detail can be missed, so compare finer layouts for mean and shape convergence. With emission, nonlinear mixing is applied before the combined light field is interpolated, so it can differ from mixing separately interpolated media fields. The field layout may be refined by the interior's prepared-media resolution or refraction detail; changing absorption or scattering coefficients does not change the light's normalization.

Power normalization and the source proposal use this same prepared RGB target. Four independently stratified volume estimates use 163, 243 and at most 323 points each, targeting at most 0.25% relative discrepancy. Unresolved variation is reported; nonzero lumens require a positive integral. Positive nits with an unresolved power integral are rejected rather than published with zero source-selection weight. Half the position proposal remains uniform over the bounding box, preserving support of the prepared target. Every sample divides by its complete proposal probability.

At every distance, receivers integrate the finite volume rather than a centre point or a silhouette disk. Direction-and-chord sampling keeps the near field finite without a cell-distance clamp. Distant inverse-square divergence and the narrowing angular penumbra follow the same source. The samples count controls integration noise and shadow quality, not expected brightness. Increasing it costs more source evaluations and shadow rays; it is not an anti-aliasing setting.

Prepared method 4 media sample camera distance and source position jointly. For each light, samples sets the number of pairs for one geometric ray segment, shared across its numerical refraction chords and media integration calls. Each pair uses the local scattering coefficient, phase function, camera transmission and source-connection transmission, divided by both sampling probabilities. Optical depth and authored glow retain their own integration steps; refining those steps does not multiply the source-ray count. A recursive continuation through a surface starts another segment, so this is not a cap on an entire reflected or refracted ray tree. Explicitly selected legacy media retain their coefficient evaluators; their volume-light connections use the same separate path budget. Console statistics report collected legs, cells and attempted source pairs, plus the maximum cells per leg and an estimate of live sampling scratch space across nested legs on one render thread; allocator overhead and retained pool storage are not included.

  • Surface receivers apply their BSDF and incidence cosine. Scattering media use their scattering coefficient and phase function, without a surface cosine. Both receive the same source distribution.
  • With media_attenuation, shadow rays integrate media along the path, including media inside the emitting container. These coefficients affect transport, never source generation.
  • shadowless disables surface occlusion; media_interaction off excludes this light from scattering-media illumination.
  • Photon method 2 samples the same emission coefficient and carries physical flux; surface irradiance conversion and volume scattering use their respective transport measures.
  • Shadow rays remain straight through refracting media. Fully bent direct-light paths and the final refractive-index radiance convention remain limitations; this is not a claim of exact refractive transport.

Finite source geometry, pigment and visibility are integrated at every distance. Scene examples expose Declare=Samples=N to compare quality at different draw counts.

3.12.4 Media, Light Groups and Radiosity

An interior can also contain an independent refraction { function { DeltaN } detail D } block beside its light and coefficient media. The refraction field changes the local index without changing source generation or the media density; its detail is in scene units. A direct field function is a raw signed scalar, while the light's pigment remains a colour source.

  • The glow. The camera, reflections and refractions see authored media emission. Radiosity and photon transport exclude that appearance term while retaining light genuinely scattered by the medium; the independent light block supplies generated energy.
  • Light groups. An object with a light interior inside a light_group lights the objects of that group only, as the group's light sources do. See Light Groups.
  • Other media. With a pigment source, media mix and priority do not carve the light. With light { emission }, mixing of the source interior's own media also shapes its emission field. Overlapping objects never contribute to that source field. It is dimmed by other media only through media_attenuation.
  • Several lights. Every interior with a light block is its own light.
  • Copies. A declared object placed several times, scaled or moved, has one light per placement, and a radiance-anchored light renders the same at every scale.

3.12.5 Limits and Messages

  • Only an object's interior can hold a light. Atmospheric media cannot.
  • Media mixing affects the light source only when emission selects the interior's own media emission.
  • A light interior's photons are shot by photon method 2; photon method 1 ignores its photons block with a warning.
  • Volume lights are not yet seen through portals.
A light block has no fade_power; its falloff is attenuation <power>, <scale>.Error: fade_power in a light block. Likewise fade_distance.
A light block's brightness must be finite and not negative.Error.
Interior light ...Error before tracing: the source has unsupported bounds or ray context, invalid source-field values, or an unresolved positive power integral. Valid functions that require serial preparation are evaluated before tracing. Absorption and scattering remain independent of source generation.
photons in an interior light are ignored by photon method 1.Warning.

3.12.6 Worked Examples

All of these are in scenes/interior/media and take the options named in their first comment lines with Declare=.

3.12.6.1 A Candle in a Misty Room

blend_candle.pov is the flame of 3.12.1 in a room filled with mist. The flame and its smoke add to the mist, so they use mix add; the mist and smoke dim the flame's light on its way to the surfaces, because media_attenuation is on. Declare=FlameLight=0 swaps the media light for a point light at the wick, to compare, and FlameSamples sets the samples.

3.12.6.2 Tubes and Neon

light_tubes.pov lights an underground passage only by fluorescent tubes and neon strips. A tube is a thin transparent cylinder with a glowing medium, and a fine resolution so the grid follows its thin shape; one tube is dying and one is dead, by the strength of the light.

cylinder {
  <0, 0, -Length / 2>, <0, 0, Length / 2>, 0.028
  pigment { rgbt 1 }
  interior { media { mix add method 4 resolution 0.009 } light { pigment { rgb Colour * 180 * Strength } samples Samples } }
  translate P
}

3.12.6.3 Glow Inside Other Media

light_clouds.pov lets sky lanterns drift into a cumulus and light it from inside, and a flash light a storm tower from within. light_explosion.pov shades a fireball's light with its own smoke and warps the street behind it with the heat. light_fish.pov makes glowing fish and jellyfish the only light in water that dims and tints it (Declare=Murk=0 clears the water). In each, the light is stated as radiance in the interior's light block, and the surrounding media act on it through media_attenuation and, for the scattering, media_interaction.

3.12.6.4 Overlapping and Contained Lights

light_intersections.pov overlaps red, green and blue lights, threads them with a bronze knot, holds a glass ball in one and crosses them with smoke; it also seals a glow in a cracked stone and sinks a pool into the floor. light_colonnade.pov lights a rotunda only by a glowing core at its centre, with a lens of space around it made by refraction fields (Declare=Refract=0 drops it).

3.12.7 Surface Lights

Place light { ... } directly on an object to illuminate from its surface. The existing interior { light { ... } } form illuminates from the volume. Surface light requires finite, nonzero area and emits outward with a one-sided Lambert distribution.

box { <-1,2,-1>, <1,2.1,1>
  light { pigment { rgb <1,0.5,0.2> } brightness 8 nits samples 16 }
}

A surface light accepts the shared pigment or bare emission input, brightness, samples, shadowless, media_interaction, media_attenuation and photon reflection/refraction options. It does not accept the volume light's artistic attenuation setting. With no input the source is white; brightness defaults to 1 nit. Unitless brightness and nits set the emitted radiance to brightness times the source RGB. lumens normalizes generated flux as π times the surface integral of greyscale radiance, before media transport.

On a surface, light { emission } selects the material's pigment times finish emission. This input is mutually exclusive with an independent light pigment. The light block changes illumination without adding visible camera emission; author the material appearance separately. light, nits and lumens stay contextual in classic and 4.0 scenes at every scene version.

Preparation accepts finite nonnegative source RGB independent of ray and camera context. Material emission supports layered textures, recursive texture maps, weighted averages, UV mapping and material maps, including pigment opacity and transmission between layers. Fresnel finish attenuation and energy-conserving reflection that would alter emission by direction are rejected. See scenes/lights/surface_light.pov for a white bulb, a patterned screen, a strip and a mesh source.

Both surface and interior lights accept bounce or bounce { passes N }. Bare bounce requests one bounce order. passes must be a finite positive integer in the unsigned range. Duplicate bounce or passes settings are errors. With no explicit pigment or emission input, a bounce-enabled light selects its own emission field; zero emission stays zero. An explicit input may appear before or after bounce. The words bounce and passes are contextual and remain available as identifiers elsewhere.

Bounce preparation advances exact scattering orders rather than repeatedly feeding accumulated light back into the same pass. For source l, let J0,l be authored emission and D0,l = J0,l. Order k is Dk,l = Kl[ΣmDk−1,m] while k is within that source's passes, and zero afterward. Final output is authored emission plus the sum of completed orders. A source that reaches its cutoff contributes no new order; its older orders are not emitted again. Authored sources and ordinary lights enter generation zero once. Brightness and lumen normalization apply only to authored primary emission, never to received indirect energy.

Volume bounce preparation shares the medium's prepared spatial layout. Isotropic and Rayleigh scattering retain their angular form; HG and Mie scattering use positive, per-RGB weighted reservoirs bounded by the existing samples budget. Each cached realization preserves the RGB angular mean, and directional estimates remain conditionally unbiased Monte Carlo estimates with independent streams by pass, node and source. Spatial evaluation interpolates the cached field with convex weights. The diffuse source excludes glossy and ballistic transport. Surface bounce rejects stochastic crand diffuse finishes as well as the direction-dependent finish attenuation described above. Source-sample visibility and extinction on the final path to a receiver are still evaluated during rendering.
