3.12 Surface and Volume Lights

An object can be declared a light, either from its surface or from its volume. A light block on the object (Surface Lights) makes its surface a one-sided Lambertian emitter; a light block in its interior makes its volume an emitter. Either one can also scatter on the light it receives (Bounced and Passive Lights) and is stated in nits or lumens (Brightness Units and scale_lumens). The rest of this chapter first describes the volume light.

A volume of medium can be declared a light. It lights the surfaces and scattering media around it, casts shadows shaped by its own volume, and states its own colour and brightness in a light block beside the media, separate from the glow the camera sees. A candle flame, a neon tube, a fireball, a glowing jellyfish and a lantern inside a cloud are all one interior with a light block.

This chapter describes the block, the radiance law that gives the light its power and falloff, how it is sampled, how it meets light groups, radiosity and other media, and its limits. The media themselves are described in Emission and Density Drives the Coefficients. The sample scenes light_*.pov and blend_candle.pov in scenes/interior/media are the worked examples. The examples in this chapter assume #version 4.0;.

3.12.1 Making a Volume a Light

A light statement inside an interior statement makes the object an independent volume light. The word light is matched by its text before a block, so it stays free as an identifier everywhere else. The block belongs to the interior, not to any one medium: one interior, one light, using the object's shape and its own pigment or the combined emission of its own media. An interior declared as an identifier, or copied, keeps its light.

INTERIOR_LIGHT:
  light { [INTERIOR_LIGHT_ITEM...] }
INTERIOR_LIGHT_ITEM:
  pigment { PIGMENT } | emission | brightness Value [nits | lumens] |
  attenuation { [power Value] [scale Value] [cutoff Value] } | samples Integer |
  domain_cells Integer |
  shadowless [Bool] | media_interaction [Bool] | media_attenuation [Bool] |
  photons { [reflection Bool] [refraction Bool] } | bounce [{ [passes Integer] [self Float | Vector | rgb Vector | on | off] }]

Interior light default values:

pigment          : solid white
emission         : absent (use pigment)
brightness       : 1.0 nits
attenuation      : power 2, scale 1 (physical), cutoff 0.000004
samples          : 64
domain_cells     : the scene's light default (500000, #default { interior { light { domain_cells N } } })
shadowless       : off
media_interaction: on
media_attenuation: on
pigmentThe light's own colour or pattern, evaluated in the container's coordinates. The pattern distributes emitted light independently of media density. Default solid white.
emissionA bare flag selecting the full combined RGB emission of this interior's own media, including density patterns and media mixing, as the light's emission coefficient. It takes no value and cannot be combined with pigment. Absorption, scattering and overlapping objects do not supply source colour. A unitless brightness multiplies the media's own emission, so brightness 1 casts the same emission coefficient the camera integrates as glow; nits scale it to a stated mean radiance at the container's boundary and lumens to a stated flux.
brightnessA non-negative source brightness. Unitless, it multiplies the input: a pigment through the topology-normalized radiance convention below, emission as a plain multiplier of the media's emission. nits state the input's measured mean radiance at the container's boundary, whatever its colour or pattern; lumens specifies total generated luminous flux under the convention 1 scene unit = 1 m. In a bounce-only block it multiplies the bounced light instead (see Bounced and Passive Lights). See Radiance, Attenuation and Cutoff.
attenuationThe falloff: a block of a power, a scale and a cutoff, each given at most once and 2, 1 and 0.000004 by default; power 2 and scale 1 are physical. A scale of 0 turns the falloff off; a cutoff of 0 turns the cutoff off. See Radiance, Attenuation and Cutoff.
samplesAn integer of at least 1: emitter draws per lit point, and so the shadow rays. More samples give smoother soft shadows and cost more. Varying method 4 media take √samples × their light_samples draws per cell width of ray instead; a surface takes samples × its finish's light_samples. See Sampling and Shadows.
domain_cellsAn integer from 1 to 1000000000: the cell count of this light's prepared source domain, the lattice the light is sampled from, which also lays out its prepared light field and the gather grid of a bounced volume. Without it the light takes the scene's light default, 500000 cells, set with #default { interior { light { domain_cells N } } } for lights parsed after it. A light never takes its medium's domain_cells: preparing the light is usually the most expensive part and its source need not resolve what the medium's density does, so the two are set apart. The lattice targets N cells over the container's bounding box, with at most 128 × ∛(max(1, N/500000)) cells along any axis; counts round up to whole cells, so it is a target, not a strict ceiling. Given at most once. On a surface light, the count budgets surface nodes instead (see What Bounce Costs). The word is matched by its text and stays free as an identifier.
shadowlessThe light casts no shadows, as a shadowless light does.
media_interactionWith off, the light does not light scattering media, as for a normal light source.
media_attenuationWith off, a shadow ray is not dimmed by the media it passes; see Atmospheric Attenuation.
bounceLets the interior's scattering media scatter on the light they receive and emit it as further orders. See Bounced and Passive Lights.
photonsPhoton options for the light: reflection and refraction switches, as a light source's photons block. The scene photon configuration and collecting targets remain necessary; these switches select reflected and refracted photon routes.

The block takes no position. The light is placed, transformed and copied with its object. The container must be finite. This example authors a separate visible glow; matching its colour does not make its angular radiance equal to the cast light:

sphere {
  0, 1 pigment { rgbt 1 }
  interior {
    media { method 4 emission rgb <14, 6, 1.6> * 9 density { spherical color_map { [0 rgb 0] [0.4 rgb 0.3] [1 rgb 1] } } }
    light { pigment { rgb <14, 6, 1.6> * 9 } samples 32 }
  }
  scale <0.06, 0.16, 0.06> translate <0, 1.45, 0>
}

3.12.1.1 Using Media Emission as the Source

The bare emission flag reuses the media emission field without repeating its colour or density pattern. It is available at every scene version and in .pov4. Without it, the light still defaults to solid white. For example:

interior {
  media { method 4 emission rgb <100, 40, 10> density { spherical } }
  light { emission brightness 12 lumens }
}

This generates 12 lumens using the combined RGB emission field as its distribution. The media's visible glow is unchanged. A nonzero lumen setting requires a positive source integral; absent or zero emission cannot normalize it. See scenes/interior/media/light_emission.pov.

Without a unit, brightness is a gain on the media's emission: the light's emission coefficient is j(x) = B ε(x), where ε(x) is the combined emission of the interior's media at x and B the brightness. Nothing depends on the size or shape of the container. For example:

// the light is the glow: each point emits its media emission times brightness
sphere { 0, 0.3
  pigment { rgbt 1 }
  interior {
    media { method 4 emission rgb <4, 1.5, 0.4> density { spherical } }
    light { emission brightness 1 samples 24 }
  }
  translate y*1.5
}

casts the emission coefficient the camera integrates as the sphere's glow. brightness 2 casts twice that, without changing the glow. brightness 2 nits instead states what the glow measures: the emission field is scaled so that its mean outgoing radiance over the container's boundary is 2 nits. A volume of total intensity ∫j dV radiating through a boundary of area A has the Lambertian-equivalent mean radiance 4∫j dV/A, so the light casts j(x) = B ε(x) A/(4∫Y(ε) dV), integrated over the prepared field, density patterns and mixing included. Its strength no longer follows the media's emission: scaling every emission coefficient by the same factor leaves the light unchanged. A pigment in nits is normalized the same way, as described next.

3.12.2 Radiance, Attenuation and Cutoff

The light uses its declared emitting region and source field. By default that field is its own pigment, independent of media density. With light { emission }, it is instead the full combined emission RGB of the same interior's media, after density and media mixing. Absorption, scattering, refraction and other objects do not contribute to this source field. Let V be the region's geometric volume, A its true boundary area and C(x) the selected source field. For a pigment source, a unitless brightness uses reference length ℓ = 4V/A and isotropic emission coefficient j(x) = L C(x)/ℓ, so a white pigment's boundary radiance is L and a coloured or patterned one's is L times its colour. Exposed cavity walls contribute to A; internal union seams do not. This is not a convex hull or projected silhouette normalization. The declared emitting region affects this reference calibration: padding its container while keeping a smaller pigment field unchanged is not guaranteed to preserve source strength. For an emission source, a unitless brightness needs no reference length: j(x) = L C(x), the media's own emission coefficient times the brightness, so the container's size and padding do not change the strength. With nits, either source field is normalized to the stated mean boundary radiance, j(x) = L C(x) ℓ−1 V/∫Y(C) dV with the same ℓ = 4V/A, both integrals taken over the same prepared draws, and a constant field's mean luminance taken exactly: a uniform C casts exactly the unitless j of the colour C/Y(C), so a white pigment reads the same with or without the unit. Nonzero nits over a field whose luminance integrates to zero cannot be normalized and are an error.

A uniform white pigment source generates flux πLA. This is a predictable volume-source reference, not identical radiance along every chord or identical illumination at every nearby point. With no absorbing or scattering media and a white Lambertian probe of unit reflectance, a uniform unit cube gives about 0.9257L at its centre with an axis-aligned normal and 1.2399L immediately outside a face centre facing inward. A sphere gives 0.75L at its centre and L immediately outside facing inward. Geometry, receiver orientation and pigment determine actual illumination; there is no cell-sized near-distance clamp.

lumens specifies generated flux Φ, before absorption on the way out. It normalizes j(x) = ΦC(x)/(4π∫Y(C(x)) dV), where Y is the renderer's existing greyscale convention (RGB weights 0.297, 0.589 and 0.114, with white equal to one). A pattern redistributes the stated total; uniformly multiplying a nonzero pigment does not change that total. This is the renderer's established luminance convention, not a new spectral or colour-management model. Nonzero lumens over a field whose luminance integrates to zero are an error; a positive field whose sampled integral is still zero, a narrow source missed by preparation, reports an unresolved integral instead.

The volume emitter is the reference model. An authored surface emitter can be an approximation with somewhat different angular and near-field illumination; exact surface/volume identity is not promised. A transparent emitting volume is also not automatically an opaque monitor or a frosted lamp envelope. Authors choose source geometry, backing and any separate scattering diffuser to represent those objects; no geometric angular multiplier silently turns a transparent source into a diffuse surface.

For nits, uniformly scaling geometry and receiver positions by s divides j by s, multiplies generated flux by s2, and preserves illumination. At fixed lumens, j instead scales by 1/s3 and illumination by 1/s2. To preserve the optical appearance of coexisting media, their inverse-length absorption and scattering coefficients must also scale by 1/s; resizing the same physical material is a different operation. With emission and a unitless brightness, j follows the media's emission coefficient, which the author scales with the other coefficients; with nits or lumens it is absolute, as for a pigment. No light unit reads mm_per_unit. Lumens assume one scene unit is one metre; scale_lumens(Phi, c) returns Phi / c2 for author-stated metres per scene unit c.

Physical divergence follows the finite-source integral and approaches inverse-square behavior at large distance. attenuation { power Power scale Scale } adds an artistic, nonconservative path modifier. Write p for Power, a for Scale, R for the bounding radius, d for source-to-receiver path length and q = R/max(d,R). For positive a the modifier is qp−2aqp−1. The default, power 2 and scale 1, is exactly one. At distances within R it stays one; with power 2 its distant limit is 1/a. A zero scale compensates distant inverse-square divergence. Since physical divergence falls as 1/d2 far away, the far field falls as 1/dp: a power below 2 makes the light reach further. The same modifier applies to direct and photon paths. Classic fade_power and fade_distance are not accepted in this block.

cutoff leaves a light out at a lit point where the most it could add there is fainter than the cutoff, on the 0 to 1 output scale of adc_bailout: that point draws no positions from the light and traces no shadow rays to it. The bound is the light's prepared power, falling by the law above, inverse-square within R, from the nearest point of the light's bounding box, so it holds for every light, pattern and shape, and does not change when the whole scene is scaled. The default, 0.000004, is about a thousandth of the default adc_bailout: many lights that are each faint over most of a scene cost only where they are bright, and the image does not visibly change. cutoff 0 turns it off. Surface lights take the cutoff too, as attenuation { cutoff Value }, and the default applies to every surface, volume and bounced light. It applies to surface shading, media scattering, subsurface scattering and bounce preparation alike.

The medium's authored emission is appearance alone: camera, reflection and refraction rays see it, but it creates no radiosity or photon energy. Light genuinely scattered by the medium remains eligible for indirect transport. A lantern's visible glow comes from its haze scattering the light, or from an authored emission look. Use light { emission } to reuse the glow's RGB field as the light input. With no unit the cast emission coefficient is then the media's own times the brightness, so brightness 1 casts the coefficient the camera integrates as glow.

3.12.2.1 The Attenuation Block

attenuation { [power Value] [scale Value] [cutoff Value] }

Each of the three items may be written once, in any order, and a light takes one block. The numbers stand inside the braces: attenuation 2 is a parse error. The defaults are the physical law and a cutoff that costs nothing visible.

powerA number greater than 0, default 2. With R the bounding radius of the light, d the path length from the source to the receiver and q = R/max(d, R), the light's physical divergence is multiplied by m = qp−2aqp−1, with p the power and a the scale. Within R the factor is 1, and far away the light falls as 1/dp: a power of 1.5 reaches further than the physical 2. Volume lights only.
scaleA number of 0 or more, default 1. The distant limit of m for power 2 is 1/a; a scale of 0 compensates the divergence, so that the light does not fall off at all. Power 2 and scale 1 give m = 1 exactly. Volume lights only.
cutoffA finite number of 0 or more, default 0.000004 (a thousandth of the default adc_bailout), 0 to turn it off. A lit point where the light could add less than this, on the 0 to 1 output scale, draws no positions from the light and traces no shadow rays to it. Surface lights, volume lights and bounced lights all take it.

The example gives a lantern whose light falls as 1/d1.5, with a cutoff ten times stricter than the default, and a panel that takes the cutoff alone, switched off:

// lantern: 400 lumens, falling as 1/d^1.5 so it reaches further, with a stricter cutoff
sphere { 0, 0.2
  pigment { rgbt 1 }
  interior { light { brightness 400 lumens attenuation { power 1.5 scale 1 cutoff 0.00001 } } }
  translate y*1.5
}
// a surface light takes the cutoff only; 0 turns it off
box { <-1, 3, -1>, <1, 3.05, 1> light { brightness 5 attenuation { cutoff 0 } } }

A surface light accepts cutoff only: power or scale in its block is the error A surface light's attenuation accepts only cutoff; power and scale are for interior lights. A surface light always follows the physical law of its geometry. fade_power and fade_distance are not items of a light block.

3.12.3 Sampling and Shadows

Preparation estimates ℓ from isotropic lines through the emitting region: occupied path length divided by the count of uninterrupted occupied intervals. Full intersection lists include cavity walls and merge internal union seams. Four independently scrambled estimates use 1024, 4096 and at most 16384 lines each, stopping when their maximum relative discrepancy is at most 0.5%. A discrepancy warning reports failure to meet that target at the limit. This is an empirical replicate check, not a certified error bound; thin geometry and narrow procedural pigment peaks can evade every preparation probe.

Preparation publishes an immutable RGB light field on the interior's shared world-space layout before tracing. Solid pigment colours remain analytic; other source fields are sampled on that layout and reconstructed by trilinear interpolation. The source geometry clips this field when light is evaluated, without masking grid nodes at curved boundaries. Runtime sampling never evaluates the authored source pattern. This sampled field approximates the authored pattern: subcell detail can be missed, so compare finer layouts for mean and shape convergence. With emission, nonlinear mixing is applied before the combined light field is interpolated, so it can differ from mixing separately interpolated media fields. The field layout follows the light's domain_cells, and may be refined by a method 4 medium's domain_cells or the refraction detail of the same interior; changing absorption or scattering coefficients does not change the light's normalization.

Power normalization and the source proposal use this same prepared RGB target. Four independently stratified volume estimates use 163, 243 and at most 323 points each, targeting at most 0.25% relative discrepancy. Unresolved variation is reported; nonzero lumens require a positive integral. Positive nits with an unresolved power integral are rejected rather than published with zero source-selection weight. Half the position proposal remains uniform over the bounding box, preserving support of the prepared target. Every sample divides by its complete proposal probability.

At every distance, receivers integrate the finite volume rather than a centre point or a silhouette disk. Direction-and-chord sampling keeps the near field finite without a cell-distance clamp. Distant inverse-square divergence and the narrowing angular penumbra follow the same source. The samples count controls integration noise and shadow quality, not expected brightness. Increasing it costs more source evaluations and shadow rays; it is not an anti-aliasing setting.

The light's samples N (default 64 for both surface and interior light blocks) says how detailed it is for every receiver, and each receiver scales it with its own light_samples M (default 1.0): a surface draws round(N × M) source positions per hit (finish), and a varying method 4 medium draws √N × M per cell width of ray (media), where the cell width is that of the finest grid among the media the ray is in. In the medium the draws are evenly spaced along the ray from an offset drawn per light, each at its own position with its own shadow ray traced through the media to the light, where the light's falloff and the medium's phase apply; at samples 64 that is 8 per cell width. A finer prepared grid (more domain_cells) therefore takes more draws, and M scales a medium's lighting cost in proportion. Constant method 4 media have no steps and sample camera distance and source position jointly instead, as do the light blocks' contributions along legacy media intervals. For each light, round(√N × M) sets the number of pairs for one geometric ray segment, shared across its numerical refraction chords and media integration calls. Each pair uses the local scattering coefficient, phase function, camera transmission and source-connection transmission, divided by both sampling probabilities. Optical depth and authored glow retain their own integration steps; refining those steps does not multiply the source-ray count. A recursive continuation through a surface starts another segment, so this is not a cap on an entire reflected or refracted ray tree. Legacy media methods 1 to 3 do not scatter a light block's light; see Light blocks and classic transport under Limits and Messages. Console statistics report collected legs, cells and attempted source pairs, plus the maximum cells per leg and an estimate of live sampling scratch space across nested legs on one render thread; allocator overhead and retained pool storage are not included.

  • Surface receivers apply their BSDF and incidence cosine. Scattering media use their scattering coefficient and phase function, without a surface cosine. Both receive the same source distribution.
  • With media_attenuation, shadow rays integrate media along the path, including media inside the emitting container. These coefficients affect transport, never source generation.
  • shadowless disables surface occlusion; media_interaction off excludes this light from scattering-media illumination.
  • Photon method 2 samples the same emission coefficient and carries physical flux; surface irradiance conversion and volume scattering use their respective transport measures.
  • Shadow rays remain straight through refracting media. Fully bent direct-light paths and the final refractive-index radiance convention remain limitations; this is not a claim of exact refractive transport.

Finite source geometry, pigment and visibility are integrated at every distance. Scene examples expose Declare=Samples=N to compare quality at different draw counts.

3.12.4 Media, Light Groups and Radiosity

An interior can also contain an independent refraction { function { DeltaN } detail D } block beside its light and coefficient media. The refraction field changes the local index without changing source generation or the media density; its detail is in scene units. A direct field function is a raw signed scalar, while the light's pigment remains a colour source.

  • The glow. The camera, reflections and refractions see authored media emission. Radiosity and photon transport exclude that appearance term while retaining light genuinely scattered by the medium; the independent light block supplies generated energy.
  • Light groups. An object with a light interior inside a light_group lights the objects of that group only, as the group's light sources do. See Light Groups.
  • Other media. With a pigment source, media mix and priority do not carve the light. With light { emission }, mixing of the source interior's own media also shapes its emission field. Overlapping objects never contribute to that source field. It is dimmed by other media only through media_attenuation.
  • Several lights. Every interior with a light block is its own light.
  • Copies. A declared object placed several times, scaled or moved, has one light per placement, and a radiance-anchored light renders the same at every scale.

3.12.5 Limits and Messages

  • Only an object's interior can hold a light. Atmospheric media cannot.
  • Media mixing affects the light source only when emission selects the interior's own media emission.
  • A light interior's photons are shot by photon method 2; photon method 1 ignores its photons block with a warning.
  • Interior and surface lights are not imaged by portals: they light what they reach directly, and a portal does not carry their light to its far mouth.
  • A light_source cannot hold a light block.
  • Light blocks and classic transport. Media methods 1 to 3 and subsurface method 1 stay exactly as they were, so they take no part in the light of a light block: they cannot make it, scatter it, bounce it or carry it beneath a surface. The parse stops at its end, with a message naming the shape, the medium or finish and the light by the lines they are written on, in three cases:
    • the interior that holds a light block also holds a method 1 to 3 medium of any kind, scattering or not (a surface light puts no such limit on its object's own media);
    • a method 1 to 3 medium that scatters, in an object or in the atmosphere, in a scene where some light block lights media (media_interaction on, the default);
    • a subsurface method 1 finish, whether it names the method or takes the scene's (method 1 before version 4.0), on an object with a light block, or on any object in a scene with a light block, since every light block lights surfaces.
    A method 1 to 3 medium that only absorbs or glows stays legal everywhere else: the light of light blocks is dimmed by it and the camera sees its glow. Use method 4 (the default from version 4.0) and subsurface method 2 (likewise) with light blocks.

For example, a method 4 medium inside the light's interior and a classic medium that only absorbs elsewhere are legal:

  pigment { rgbt 1 }
  interior { media { method 4 scattering { 1, rgb 0.2 } } light { brightness 20 } }
  translate y*1.5
}
box { <-3, 0, 1>, <3, 2, 3> pigment { rgbt 1 }
  interior { media { method 3 absorption rgb 0.05 } }
}

A classic medium in the light's own interior stops the parse:

sphere { 0, 0.3
  pigment { rgbt 1 }
  interior { media { method 3 scattering { 1, rgb 0.2 } } light { brightness 20 } }
}

with The sphere light block at line L of F holds the method 3 medium at line N of F; a light block needs method 4 media. A classic scattering medium anywhere in a scene whose light lights media stops it as well, unless every light block says media_interaction off:

// the surface light lights media, so a classic scattering medium anywhere stops the parse
box { <-1, 3, -1>, <1, 3.05, 1> light { brightness 5 } }
box { <-3, 0, 1>, <3, 2, 3> pigment { rgbt 1 }
  interior { media { method 3 scattering { 1, rgb 0.05 } } }
}

// media_interaction off on the light lets a classic scattering medium stay in the scene
box { <-1, 3, -1>, <1, 3.05, 1> light { brightness 5 media_interaction off } }
box { <-3, 0, 1>, <3, 2, 3> pigment { rgbt 1 }
  interior { media { method 3 scattering { 1, rgb 0.05 } } }
}

The first gives The method 3 medium at line N of F in the box scatters, and the box light block at line L of F lights media; only method 4 media scatter a light block's light. The second is accepted.

An item of a nested block is accepted only inside its block, in both syntaxes. reflection written directly in a light block is the error 'reflection' belongs inside the photons block of a light., and the same holds for the items of other blocks:

box { <-1, 3, -1>, <1, 3.05, 1> light { brightness 5 reflection on } }

box { <-1, 3, -1>, <1, 3.05, 1> light { brightness 5 photons { reflection on } } }

3.12.6 Worked Examples

All of these are in scenes/interior/media and take the options named in their first comment lines with Declare=.

3.12.6.1 A Candle in a Misty Room

blend_candle.pov is the flame of 3.12.1 in a room filled with mist. The flame and its smoke add to the mist, so they use mix add; the mist and smoke dim the flame's light on its way to the surfaces, because media_attenuation is on. 3.12.6.3 Glow Inside Other Media

light_clouds.pov uses 64 samples per light by default (Declare=Samples=N) and lets nine sky lanterns drift into a cumulus and light it from inside, and a blue lightning flash light a storm tower from within. The lightning keeps its previous luminance while shifting toward blue with a little red. Four lanterns sit along the cumulus's lower-right edge facing the tower; Declare=Bounce=0 disables only the clouds' bounced light for a matching comparison. The cumulus uses two bounce orders with brightness 48 (Declare=BounceBrightness=N) and self share 0.00625 (Declare=SelfBounce=N), so its outgoing warm light is strong without a large gain in its own appearance. In each, the light is stated in its interior's light block, in nits, as a gain over the media's own emission, or in lumens, and the surrounding media act on it through media_attenuation and, for the scattering, media_interaction.

3.12.6.4 Overlapping and Contained Lights

3.12.7 Surface Lights

A light block placed directly on an object makes the object's surface a light. Every point of the surface emits outward with a one-sided Lambertian distribution over the object's real geometry, so a box, a mesh, a CSG shape or a stretched sphere is the lamp itself, with the soft shadows and the near and far illumination of its true shape. The block works on any object with finite surface of nonzero area, at every scene version. The word light is matched by its text before a block, so it stays free as an identifier.

SURFACE_LIGHT:
  light { [SURFACE_LIGHT_ITEM...] }
SURFACE_LIGHT_ITEM:
  pigment { PIGMENT } | emission | brightness Value [nits | lumens] |
  attenuation { cutoff Value } |
  domain_cells Integer | samples Integer | shadowless [Bool] | media_interaction [Bool] |
  media_attenuation [Bool] | photons { [reflection Bool] [refraction Bool] } |
  bounce [{ passes Integer }]

Surface light default values:

pigment          : solid white
emission         : absent (use pigment)
brightness       : 1.0 nits
attenuation      : cutoff 0.000004
samples          : 64
domain_cells     : 4096 (surface nodes)
shadowless       : off
media_interaction: on
media_attenuation: on
bounce           : absent
pigmentThe colour or pattern the surface emits, evaluated at each point of the surface. It is the light's own colour and is independent of the object's texture. Default solid white. It cannot depend on the ray or the camera, so aoi and similar patterns are rejected.
emissionA bare flag that copies the material's own emission: the object's texture pigment times its finish emission, evaluated at each point, including layered textures, texture_map and material_map patterns, UV mapping and pigment transparency between layers. It takes no value and cannot be combined with pigment. Where the material has no emission the surface emits nothing.
brightnessA finite, non-negative number, optionally followed by a unit. Unitless, it multiplies the input: the radiance is the value times the pigment, or times the material's emission. nits state the input's mean radiance over the surface, L(x) = B C(x) A/∫Y(C) dA, whatever its colour or pattern. lumens states the total luminous flux. In a bounce-only block it multiplies the bounced light instead. See Brightness Units and scale_lumens.
attenuationOnly cutoff: where the light could add less than this at a lit point it is left out there, as for a volume light; see Radiance, Attenuation and Cutoff. 0.000004 by default, 0 off.
samplesAn integer of at least 1: surface points drawn per lit point, and so the shadow rays. It also sets the incident-light draws per node when preparing this surface light's bounce field. More samples reduce sampling noise and cost more; each draw keeps the expected incident contribution. See What Bounce Costs.
shadowlessThe light casts no shadows, as a shadowless light does.
media_interactionWith off, the light does not light scattering media.
media_attenuationWith off, a shadow ray is not dimmed by the media it passes.
photonsreflection and refraction switches selecting the photon routes of this light, as a light source's photons block. Only photon method 2 shoots them.
bounceAdds light the surface itself scatters on. See Bounced and Passive Lights.
domain_cellsSurface nodes for bounce and exported subsurface light, including seeds and refinement, independently of the volume default. Integer 1 to 1000000000; default 4096, set with #default { light { domain_cells N } }. See What Bounce Costs and scenes/lights/surface_domain_cells.pov.

The light is placed, transformed and copied with its object, and takes part in light_group as any other light. An object does not light itself from a convex surface: a sphere's own light never reaches the sphere. The light does not make the object visible; the glow the camera sees is the object's own material, which is authored separately, usually with a finish emission as below.

box { <-0.6, 1, -0.05>, <0.6, 2, 0.05>
  pigment { checker rgb <0.1, 0.3, 1> rgb <1, 0.15, 0.02> scale 0.3 }
  finish { ambient 0 emission 1 diffuse 0 }
  light { emission brightness 10 nits samples 32 }
}

Here the panel's checker pattern is both what the camera sees and what lights the room, at a mean radiance of 10 nits over the panel whatever the checker's colours. A surface light stated in lumens spreads its total flux over its whole surface, so the radiance follows from the area; scale_lumens (see Brightness Units and scale_lumens) states it for a scene drawn in another unit:

// 800 lumens spread over the panel, whatever its size
box { <-0.6, 1, -0.05>, <0.6, 2, 0.05>
  pigment { rgb 1 } finish { ambient 0 emission 1 diffuse 0 }
  light { brightness 800 lumens samples 64 }
}
// an 806 lumen panel in a scene drawn in centimetres (0.01 metre per unit)
box { <-30, 100, -2>, <30, 160, 2>
  pigment { rgb 1 } finish { ambient 0 emission 1 diffuse 0 }
  light { brightness scale_lumens(806, 0.01) lumens }
}

scenes/lights/surface_light.pov shows a bulb, a patterned screen, a strip stated in lumens and a mesh, as four quarters of one image at 4, 16, 64 and 256 samples per light.

Not accepted in a surface light block: the attenuation power and scale (a surface light always follows the physical law of its geometry, with no artistic falloff) and fade_power or fade_distance. A light_source cannot carry a light block. A surface light combines with ordinary lights, media lights and radiosity; it does not shine through portals, see below.

Accepted: crand, fresnel and ray-dependent patterns in the material of an object whose light states its own pigment; they affect how the object looks, not what it emits. A surface light on a union that holds a portal is accepted; the portal body renders nothing and neither emits nor bounces light.

Surface and volume lights are not imaged by portals. A portal diverts the light of ordinary light sources to the far mouth; the light of an object's light block, surface or interior, lights only what it can reach directly, on the side of the portal where it stands.

3.12.8 Bounced and Passive Lights

bounce makes the object, its surface or its interior's scattering media, re-emit the light it receives from other lights. emission (the material's or medium's own emission) or pigment gives a light block its own source. A block may have either or both: light { emission bounce } shines its own emission and re-emits what it receives; light { bounce } re-emits only what it receives, a passive source such as a wall lit by a lamp.

BOUNCE:
  bounce | bounce { [passes Integer] [self Float | Vector | rgb Vector | on | off] }
bounceOne bounce order: the received light, re-emitted once.
passes NThe number of orders, a finite positive integer in the unsigned range (default 1). Order k re-emits the light the sources of order k−1 give the object. Each pass adds dimmer light and preparation time.
self ScaleThe share of the bounce that reaches the object that bounced it: a scalar or RGB vector, each channel any finite value (default on, which is 1; off is 0). See Self illumination below.

A block with neither an input nor bounce is a solid white pigment light, as everywhere in this chapter. With an input, brightness and its unit state the input's emission and the bounced orders keep their physical strength. In a passive block brightness is a plain multiplier of every order it re-emits, default 1 (the physical amount): at brightness 0.5 it sends on half of what it receives, and halves again what it receives back from itself in a later pass. nits and lumens state an input's emission, so a passive block rejects them. A white emitter that also bounces writes pigment { rgb 1 }; an emitting material or medium that also bounces writes emission. The input may stand before or after bounce; bounce, passes and self are matched by their text and stay free as identifiers elsewhere.

A passive floor re-emitting two orders, and a glowing cloud that shines 3 lumens of its own and re-emits two orders:

box { <-1.5, -0.1, -1.5>, <1.5, 0, 1.5>
  pigment { rgb <0.8, 0.8, 0.8> } finish { ambient 0 emission 0 diffuse 0.8 }
  light { bounce { passes 2 } samples 8 }
}

sphere { 0, 1 pigment { rgbt 1 }
  interior {
    media { method 4 scattering { 1, rgb 0.3 } emission rgb 2 }
    light { emission brightness 3 lumens bounce { passes 2 } samples 8 }
  }
}

Surfaces. A surface re-emits the diffuse part of the light it receives, by its finish diffuse, brilliance and colour.

Subsurface. With subsurface { method 2 } (the default from #version 4.0) a surface also re-emits its subsurface light, so a skin or wax object lights its neighbours with the glow that comes through it. That light travels through the material only, not across air between facing parts of one object. A volume light buried in a closed method 2 solid lights it from within, and a bounce block on the solid exports the light leaving its surface in the first order, as light { emission } does; see Lights Inside Subsurface Materials.

Volumes. An interior { light { bounce } } re-emits the light its scattering media scatter, by their scattering coefficients and phase functions (isotropic, Rayleigh, Henyey-Greenstein, Mie).

Self illumination. self sets the share of its own bounce an object receives, any finite value per channel: self 0.05 keeps five percent, self <0.1, 0.03, 0> or self rgb <0.1, 0.03, 0> sets red, green and blue separately, and the default on keeps it all; values above 1 amplify the bounce and values below 0 subtract it from the received light. Every other object and medium receives the whole bounce, and the object's own emission lights it in full.

A volume's share is what its container's own media scatter, on camera rays and in its next pass's gather, so with self off its later passes carry only light from other bounced objects and a lone cloud keeps its look without bounce while it brightens its neighbours. The container is identified by object, not position: another container's overlapping media scatter the whole bounce. In a subsurface method 2 container, the light diffusing within carries the light's own emission and the self share of its bounce.

A surface's share is what its own object, every part of a CSG or mesh carrying the light, receives on camera rays, beneath the surface with subsurface method 2, through progressive photons and in its next pass's gather. Only shapes that face themselves, such as a bowl, a room or a composite object, light themselves, so the setting matters there.

box { <-27, 12, -2>, <3, 33, 24> pigment { rgbt 1 }
  interior {
    media { scattering { 5, rgb 0.3 } density { bozo } }
    light { samples 64 bounce { passes 1 self off } }
  }
}

scenes/interior/media/bounce_self_mirror.pov shows a concave mirror returning an interior light's original warm emission at self 0; compare Declare=Photons=0 and Declare=Photons=1 for the returned light, or Declare=Self=0 and Declare=Self=1 for the added self bounce. Saved progressive photon maps use format version 4, which holds the original and bounced light's self shares; regenerate maps saved by older builds.

Preparation. Orders are prepared one after another, each sampling the sources' previous order. A source adds orders up to its passes or its cutoff, whichever comes first, and emits its authored emission plus each completed order once; authored sources and ordinary lights enter generation zero once. A volume gathers each order at its medium's prepared layout nodes that scattering reaches: per light, round(8 × √N × M) points across the node's cell (What Bounce Costs), with full shadow-ray transmittance, averaged over those inside the bouncing containers and outside opaque solids. A node clusters that light into at most six directional lobes, each keeping its light and its directions' mean and spread, and each medium scatters a lobe by its phase function averaged over that spread's cone, exact for light from a few directions under any phase. The result is the order's diffuse emission, which camera rays and the next order sample like any volume light, with visibility and extinction to each receiver evaluated during rendering.

3.12.8.2 What Bounce Costs

Bounce is prepared once, before the first pixel is traced, in the bounce phase of the preparation progress lines. A scene without it pays nothing, and the cost does not grow with the image size. Each pass is one more order, prepared after the one before it, so the cost grows with passes.

Surfaces. A surface light's domain_cells budgets its two-dimensional nodes for bounce and exported subsurface light. The default is 4096, set for subsequent lights with #default { light { domain_cells N } }. It is independent of media and interior-light budgets. Each light seeds about 4096 nodes over its own area, or about half its budget when smaller. The seeds are reused by every order; additional nodes share the remaining budget equally among the orders still to prepare, including exported buried light. Adding another light leaves this light's seed density and budget unchanged.

Each order refines to at most 1/64 of its seed spacing. Nodes split where their light differs by more than 10% from their neighbours' prediction or across their disc, or where incoming light is closer than their spacing. For a buried source, the local subsurface diffusion length supplies that last scale. At the budget, the largest estimated errors receive nodes first. Surface colour and finish are evaluated where the field is read. Each node takes a fixed number of incident-light draws at each refinement level, set by the bouncing surface light's samples. A retained node is measured over its smaller disc after a split. There are at most seven levels, from the seed spacing through 1/64 of it, so each field takes at most 7 × domain_cells × samples incident-light draws. Each draw chooses one source: half its selection probability is shared uniformly among the sources, and half follows a power and distance estimate. Dividing its contribution by its selection probability preserves the expected incident contribution, and the uniform share keeps every source reachable. The draw evaluates that source's visibility and media transmission. Increasing samples reduces sampling noise; increasing domain_cells gives the field more spatial detail. The light's samples also controls how many surface points its outgoing receivers draw, scaled by their own light_samples. Geometry intersections, media integration and exported internal subsurface paths retain their own costs; the node and draw budgets do not bound the cost of those operations. Preparation counts completed sampling and subsurface gathers. A gather exceeding 30 seconds reports the object's source location; an internal walk exceeding 4096 events reports the object, channel and sample. Each order reports:

Bounce o1: 3474 nodes, 1 levels, 0.23 MiB, 0.0+0.0s
Bounce o1 levels: 3474
Bounce o1 splits: between 0, within 0, near 0

with its nodes in total and per level, the memory they take, the time of the refinement and of the final pass, and how many splits each criterion asked for.

Volumes. A bounced volume is prepared on the interior's media cells with the scattering coefficients and phase functions of its media, and it counts its progress in those cells and light samples rather than in nodes. It costs more than a surface of the same size: the example above counts millions of units at the default domain_cells, where the surface example counts thousands. Each node draws round(8 × √N × M) points from each light it gathers, where N is the bouncing light's samples and M the largest light_samples of its scattering media: 32 at samples 16, 64 at samples 64. Each node keeps six lobes whatever the phase, so these set the gather's noise and cost, not its memory. Raise light_samples on objects used as lights: a one-off preparation cost that improves everything they light (it also raises that medium's own lighting draws when rendered).

3.12.9 Brightness Units and scale_lumens

A brightness value is followed by an optional unit, written after the number; the unit words nits and lumens are read only there and stay free as identifiers.

brightness ValueA plain multiplier of the input: a pigment emits Value times its colour as radiance in nits, so a white pigment emits Value nits; emission casts Value times the material's or media's emission. In a bounce-only block it multiplies the bounced light.
brightness Value nitsRadiance in nits (candelas per square metre), measured: the input, pigment or emission, is scaled so its mean radiance is Value, over the surface for a surface light and at the container's boundary for a volume light, with luminance weighted by the grey level below. Its colour and pattern shape the light but not its mean. Radiance is fixed, so scaling an object up by s multiplies its emitting area, and the flux it delivers, by s2, and it lights more. An input whose luminance integrates to zero cannot be normalized and is an error, and so is the unit in a bounce-only block.
brightness Value lumensTotal generated luminous flux in lumens, before any absorption on the way out. The colour or pattern only shapes how that total is distributed. For a surface source the radiance is Value divided by π times the surface area times the grey level of the source colour (weights 0.297, 0.589 and 0.114 for red, green and blue). The flux is fixed, so scaling the object up by s divides its radiance by s2 while the illumination far from it stays the same.

Lumens assume that one scene unit is one metre. For a scene authored in another length, scale_lumens(Lumens, Metres) converts lumens stated for the real object: Metres is the number of metres in one scene unit, and the result is Lumens / Metres2, the value to write in the light block so that the object has the radiance of the real lamp at the scene's size. scale_lumens is a built-in float function with two arguments, available in classic scenes and in .pov4 scenes at every version. It needs a finite value and a positive finite number of metres; otherwise the parser reports scale_lumens takes finite lumens and a positive finite number of metres per unit. No light unit reads mm_per_unit.

// an 806 lumen lamp in a scene drawn in centimetres (0.01 metre per unit)
#declare Flux = scale_lumens(806, 0.01);
sphere { 0, 3 pigment { rgbt 1 } interior { light { brightness Flux lumens } } }

3.12.10 Lights Inside Subsurface Materials

A volume light block whose container overlaps a closed solid with a subsurface method 2 finish lights that material from within: a vein under skin, a wick inside wax, a lamp behind a membrane. Such a light is buried where its emission lies in the material. Its pigment or emission pattern varies independently of the host, so a branching or patterned emitter glows beneath a uniform material. The transport takes emission only from the parts of a path that lie in the material and connects only to source points before the path leaves it, so a light that merely shares a boundary with the host (a flame resting on wax) adds nothing inside it, and lights it through its surface as any other light does. No new syntax is involved: the host is an ordinary subsurface object and the source an ordinary interior light.

global_settings { mm_per_unit 1 subsurface { method 2 samples 16, 32 } }
box { <-1, -1, -1>, <1, 0, 1>   // the host: a closed subsurface solid
  pigment { rgb 0.8 }
  finish { ambient 0 diffuse 1 subsurface { translucency 0.5 } }
  interior { ior 1.4 }
}
sphere { <0, -0.35, 0>, 0.12      // the buried source
  hollow no_image pigment { rgbt 1 }
  interior { light { brightness 0.05 samples 16 } }
}

Transport. Inside the host, light follows the isotropic radiative transport equation of a homogeneous medium whose absorption and scattering coefficients are those of the subsurface material (its colour and translucency, measured through mm_per_unit). Each path from a shaded point runs back into the material: free flights follow Beer's law, each collision scatters isotropically and connects to a point of every buried light, straight segments gather the source emission along their length, and the boundary reflects with the exact dielectric Fresnel factor of the interior's ior, so light can be reflected inside, totally beyond the critical angle, before it leaves. Light leaving the surface takes the Fresnel transmission. Depth, absorption and scattering decide how much reaches the surface, so a deep source need not light the whole solid evenly. The internal walk uses the material's coefficients at the shaded point: of a flesh pigment looked up by volume_sampling its colour around that point, and of a layered texture the top layer's subsurface settings with the colour of the layers as they are seen there.

The skin. Where the subsurface block gives the flesh its own colour or pigment, the texture pigment is a skin over the flesh, and each crossing of it lets through the pigment raised to half the skin's thickness (see Subsurface Light Transport). Light from a buried source crosses that skin at every crossing of its path: once where it leaves, twice at each internal reflection off the boundary (in and back), and on the unscattered straight path through a thin wall at its far end, where the skin is also evaluated. A patterned skin therefore shades the glow wherever the light meets it. A material without its own flesh colour has no skin, and its buried light is unchanged.

Sampling budgets. Each shaded point of the host averages as many internal paths per colour channel as the second count of global_settings { subsurface { samples Diffuse, Single } } (50 by default); 0 turns internal light off. Every path connects to each buried light once per collision, so the light's own samples does not multiply this cost; it sets, as everywhere, how many points each other receiver draws from the light, including the host's surface where the light reaches it from outside, scaled there by the host finish's light_samples. Image size and anti-aliasing do not reduce the noise of internal paths; raise the internal count instead.

Light leaving the host. Buried light reaches the camera as the host's glow; to let it light other objects, give the host a surface light block. On a subsurface method 2 host that a buried volume light lights, light { emission } exports the flux that the buried sources send out through the surface: before the render, in the bounce phase of the preparation progress lines, it is gathered at nodes spread over the surface, as surface bounce is, and the surface emits it with the diffuse Fresnel angular profile of subsurface light, together with the material's own finish emission. brightness and its unit apply to that finish emission; the exported flux keeps its physical strength. A bounce block exports the same flux in its first order, beside the outside light the surface scatters on, and with both emission and bounce the buried flux is exported once. The export does not change how the host looks to the camera. It conserves flux statistically, while sharp directional detail of light passing straight through a thin wall becomes a diffuse exit. The light block's samples sets how many surface points each receiver draws and the incident-light draws per node for surface bounce preparation. The same surface-light samples count sets the internal paths per colour channel at each export node. The global subsurface single-scattering count controls camera shading and does not multiply this preparation budget.

box { <-1, -1, -1>, <1, 0, 1>
  pigment { rgb 0.8 }
  finish { ambient 0 diffuse 1 subsurface { translucency 0.5 } }
  interior { ior 1.4 }
  light { emission samples 32 }   // the buried source's light, leaving through the surface, lights the room
}

Limits.

  • A patterned emitter and a patterned skin are supported. Other media overlapping the host take no part in this solve.
  • The host must enclose a volume. In a scene with subsurface scattering, a volume light block whose container's bounding box meets a method 2 subsurface part that cannot enclose one, a disc that does not cut or a mesh with an inside_vector and an open border, stops the parse; so does a volume light block whose own container is, or contains, such a part. A mesh counts as closed when every edge is shared by an even number of triangles.
  • Subsurface emission in the finish remains a separate, artistic glow; it adds to a buried light where both are given.
The volume light block at line L of F lies within the subsurface mesh at line N of F, a mesh with an open border, which cannot enclose it: light from inside a subsurface host needs a closed solid around it.Error: the light's container box meets an open subsurface part (a disc is named the same way).
The volume light block at line L of F is on the mesh at line N of F, a mesh with an open border, which encloses no volume to emit from.Error: the light block's own container is, or contains, an open part.
Internal subsurface light found no exit from ...Error during the render: an engine fault, naming the host's shape and source line, the ray and the reason.
