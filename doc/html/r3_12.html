3.12 Volume Lights

An emitting medium can be declared a light. It then lights the surfaces and scattering media around it as well as glowing, casts shadows shaped by its own volume, and gets its brightness and colour from the same emission and density the camera sees, so the light always matches the glow. A candle flame, a neon tube, a fireball, a glowing jellyfish and a lantern inside a cloud are all one medium with a light_source block.

This chapter describes the block, how the light's power and falloff follow from the medium, how it is sampled, how it meets light groups, radiosity and other media, and its limits. The emission and density themselves are described in Emission and Density Drives the Coefficients. The sample scenes light_*.pov and blend_candle.pov in scenes/interior/media are the worked examples. The examples in this chapter assume #version 4.0;.

3.12.1 Making a Medium a Light

A light_source statement inside a media statement makes that medium a light. The block may be empty. A medium declared as an identifier, or copied, keeps its light.

MEDIA_LIGHT:
  light_source { [MEDIA_LIGHT_ITEM...] }
MEDIA_LIGHT_ITEM:
  samples Integer | brightness Value | shadowless |
  media_interaction [Bool] | media_attenuation [Bool] |
  fade_distance Value | fade_power Value

Media light default values:

samples           : 32
brightness        : 1.0
shadowless        : off
media_interaction : on
media_attenuation : on
fade_distance     : 0 (physical falloff)
fade_power        : 0 (physical falloff)
samplesAn integer of at least 1: the number of points of the medium that each lit point draws, and so the number of shadow rays. More samples give smoother soft shadows and cost more. See Sampling and Shadows.
brightnessA number, 0 or more, that scales the light without changing the glow. See Power, Brightness and Falloff.
shadowlessThe light casts no shadows, as a shadowless light does.
media_interactionWith off, the light does not light scattering media, as for a normal light source. A bare keyword means on.
media_attenuationWith off, a shadow ray is not dimmed by the media it passes; see Atmospheric Attenuation. A bare keyword means on.
fade_distance, fade_powerReplace the physical falloff with a regular light's fade, when fade_power is above 0. See Power, Brightness and Falloff.

The block takes no position or colour. Both come from the medium, which is placed, transformed and copied with its object, and an animated emission or density is taken afresh each frame. The container of the medium must be a finite object, and the medium must have a non-zero emission. For example, a candle flame:

sphere {
  0, 1 pigment { rgbt 1 }
  interior {
    media {
      mix add
      emission rgb <14, 6, 1.6> * 200
      density { spherical color_map { [0 rgb 0] [0.4 rgb 0.3] [1 rgb 1] } }
      light_source { }
    }
  }
  scale <0.06, 0.16, 0.06> translate <0, 1.45, 0>
}

3.12.2 Power, Brightness and Falloff

The medium emits equally in all directions. A piece of it of volume dV and density d has a radiant intensity of emission × d × dV, and a power of 4π times that. The light's intensity is the sum over the medium, and its colour is that sum times brightness. A medium of volume V and mean density d therefore lights like a point light of colour emission × d × V with fade_distance 0 fade_power 2, and the render tests compare them. A uniform ball of radius 0.25 and emission 50 lights a floor like

light_source { <0, 2, 0>, rgb 50 * 4 / 3 * pi * pow(0.25, 3) fade_distance 0 fade_power 2 }

Its light falls off with the square of the distance, as real light does and a light_source without fade_power does not. The distance is never taken to be less than half the diagonal of a grid cell (see Sampling and Shadows), which keeps the light finite at the edge of the medium. With fade_power above 0, the regular fade_distance and fade_power falloff replaces the physical one; fade_distance on its own changes nothing.

brightness scales the light and not the glow. It is an artistic override: the right way to make a light stronger is to make the emitter as bright as the real thing and leave brightness at 1, so the light keeps matching the glow.

A real flame is far brighter than the glow a scene usually gives it. The flame of the candle sample blend_candle.pov has its emission raised 200 times, to rgb <14, 6, 1.6> * 200, to light the room warmly, and its glow is then overexposed, as a flame is in a photograph exposed for the room. The sample does not raise brightness: a hot flame lights the room and also shows it.

3.12.3 Sampling and Shadows

When the scene is parsed, POV-Ray builds a table of the power of each cell of a grid over the container, counting only points inside it. The grid is the prepared grid of media method 4 (see Prepared Fields), at its resolution, or the grid method 4 would choose for the container. Building the table evaluates the density at up to eight points per cell. A grid over 1000000 cells is not refused: the cell width is raised by 5 per cent at a time until it fits. A container that is a deferred mesh (isosurface_mesh, skein_mesh or a .povm mesh) is built then, on the parser thread and one at a time, instead of in parallel with the other meshes.

Each lit point draws samples points from the medium, one from each of samples strata of equal power, each a compact region of the grid taken in Z-order, and traces a shadow ray to each. Within a cell, points are spread evenly over the eighths of the cell whose centres are inside the container. Shadows are therefore soft and shaped by the glow, and nearby surfaces are lit by its shape, as with area_illumination.

  • The cost is samples shadow rays per lit point. There are none where the whole medium is behind the surface, unless the finish has diffuse backlighting or the object is double_illuminate.
  • With media_attenuation, each shadow ray is attenuated by the media of every interior it passes, including the light's own medium and any smoke surrounding it, so smoke dims its flame. Shadow rays that cross media that only glow skip them.
  • shadowless casts no shadows. media_interaction off leaves scattering media unlit by this light.
  • A scattering medium is lit by one point of the light per media sample, as by an area light. Where a constant or coarser scattering medium lit by a media light shares a portion with a finer prepared medium, its scattered light is found at its own cadence; see Sampling Parameters & Methods.
  • Shadow rays travel straight, also through media refraction.

The strata have equal power, so the noise of the soft shadows falls as samples rises. The sample scenes use 16 to 32 and take the count as Declare=Samples=N.

3.12.4 Media, Light Groups and Radiosity

  • The glow. The camera, reflections and refractions still see the medium's glow. Gathers of radiosity { media on } leave out the glow of media that are lights, since that light already reaches surfaces directly.
  • Light groups. A medium in an object inside a light_group lights the objects of that group only, as the group's light sources do. See Light Groups.
  • Other media. The light follows the medium's own emission × density and ignores the mix and priority of other media: a subtract or replace medium that carves the glow does not carve the light. It is dimmed by other media only through media_attenuation.
  • Several lights. Every medium with a light_source block is its own light, also several in one object.
  • Copies. A declared object placed several times, scaled or moved, has one light per placement.

3.12.5 Limits and Messages

  • Only media in an object's interior can be lights. Atmospheric media cannot.
  • A medium that is a light must add or replace. It cannot mix subtract or mix multiply.
  • Media lights do not shoot photons. A photons block inside the light_source block is ignored, with a warning, as photons from media lights come with a later photon method.
  • They are not seen through portals.
A media light_source needs an object's interior; atmospheric media cannot be lights.Error: a light_source block in a scene-level media.
A media light_source needs a medium that adds or replaces, not one that subtracts or multiplies.Error: mix subtract or mix multiply with the block.
A media light_source's brightness must be finite and not negative.Error.
A media light_source gives no light because ...Warning, and the medium glows but does not light. The reason is one of: its emission is negative or not finite; it has no emission; its container is infinite; its container is unbounded; its density is negative or not finite; its density is zero throughout its container.
photons in a media light_source are ignored: volume lights do not shoot photons yet.Warning.

3.12.6 Worked Examples

All of these are in scenes/interior/media and take the options named in their first comment lines with Declare=.

3.12.6.1 A Candle in a Misty Room

blend_candle.pov is the flame of 3.12.1 in a room filled with mist. The flame and its smoke add to the mist, so they use mix add; the mist and smoke dim the flame's light on its way to the surfaces, because media_attenuation is on. Declare=FlameLight=0 swaps the media light for a point light at the wick, to compare, and FlameSamples sets the samples.

3.12.6.2 Tubes and Neon

light_tubes.pov lights an underground passage only by fluorescent tubes and neon strips. A tube is a thin transparent cylinder with an emitting medium, and a fine resolution so the grid follows its thin shape; one tube is dying and one is dead, by the strength of the emission.

cylinder {
  <0, 0, -Length / 2>, <0, 0, Length / 2>, 0.028
  pigment { rgbt 1 }
  interior { media { mix add method 4 resolution 0.009 emission Colour * 1200 * Strength light_source { samples Samples } } }
  translate P
}

3.12.6.3 Glow Inside Other Media

light_clouds.pov lets sky lanterns drift into a cumulus and light it from inside, and a flash light a storm tower from within. light_explosion.pov shades a fireball's light with its own smoke and warps the street behind it with the heat. light_fish.pov makes glowing fish and jellyfish the only light in water that dims and tints it (Declare=Murk=0 clears the water). In each, the light is the medium's emission, and the surrounding media act on it through media_attenuation and, for the scattering, media_interaction.

3.12.6.4 Overlapping and Contained Lights

light_intersections.pov overlaps red, green and blue lights, threads them with a bronze knot, holds a glass ball in one and crosses them with smoke; it also seals a glow in a cracked stone and sinks a pool into the floor. light_colonnade.pov lights a rotunda only by a glowing core at its centre, with a lens of space around it made by media refraction (Declare=Refract=0 drops it).
