3.12 Volume Lights

A volume of medium can be declared a light. It lights the surfaces and scattering media around it, casts shadows shaped by its own volume, and states its own colour and brightness in a light block beside the media, separate from the glow the camera sees. A candle flame, a neon tube, a fireball, a glowing jellyfish and a lantern inside a cloud are all one interior with a light block.

This chapter describes the block, the radiance law that gives the light its power and falloff, how it is sampled, how it meets light groups, radiosity and other media, and its limits. The media themselves are described in Emission and Density Drives the Coefficients. The sample scenes light_*.pov and blend_candle.pov in scenes/interior/media are the worked examples. The examples in this chapter assume #version 4.0;.

3.12.1 Making a Volume a Light

A light statement inside an interior statement makes that interior's media a light. The word light is matched by its text before a block, so it stays free as an identifier everywhere else. The block belongs to the interior, not to any one medium: one interior, one light, drawing on all of its media. An interior declared as an identifier, or copied, keeps its light.

INTERIOR_LIGHT:
  light { [INTERIOR_LIGHT_ITEM...] }
INTERIOR_LIGHT_ITEM:
  pigment { PIGMENT } | brightness Value [nits | lumens] |
  attenuation Value [, Value] | samples Integer |
  shadowless [Bool] | media_interaction [Bool] | media_attenuation [Bool] |
  fade_cutoff Value | far_radii Value | cluster_radii Value |
  shadows method Integer | photons { ... }

Interior light default values:

pigment          : solid white
brightness       : 1.0
attenuation      : 1, 2 (physical)
samples          : 16
shadowless       : off
media_interaction: on
media_attenuation: on
fade_cutoff      : 0.000004 (#version 4.0 and later), 0 (earlier versions)
far_radii        : 8
cluster_radii    : 0 (off)
shadows          : method 2
pigmentThe light's own colour or pattern, evaluated in the container's coordinates. A pattern states a provenance: each point of the volume radiates the pigment's colour there. Default solid white.
brightnessThe radiance the pigment gives: a number, 0 or more. nits after it names the unit and changes nothing, the number is the radiance itself; lumens makes the number the light's total power under the convention 1 scene unit = 1 m, shared over the container's surface area. See Radiance, Attenuation and Cutoff.
attenuationThe falloff: a scale and a power, 1, 2 by default, which is physical. A scale of 0 turns the falloff off. See Radiance, Attenuation and Cutoff.
samplesAn integer of at least 1: emitter draws per lit point in the near field, and so the shadow rays. More samples give smoother soft shadows and cost more. See Sampling and Shadows.
shadowlessThe light casts no shadows, as a shadowless light does.
media_interactionWith off, the light does not light scattering media, as for a normal light source.
media_attenuationWith off, a shadow ray is not dimmed by the media it passes; see Atmospheric Attenuation.
fade_cutoffA number, 0 or more: points the light reaches more faintly than this leave it out. 0 turns it off. See Radiance, Attenuation and Cutoff.
far_radii0 or at least 1: beyond this many of the emitter's radii, its centre stands for it with one shadow ray. 0 keeps the volume everywhere. See Sampling and Shadows.
cluster_radii0 or at least 1: with shadowless, lights with equal cluster_radii and equal attenuation light far points as one light. See Sampling and Shadows.
shadows method1 or 2. 2, the default, shares the per-light shadow cache; 1 walks every shadow ray of this light on its own.
photonsPhoton options for the light: reflection and refraction switches, as a light source's photons block. Photon method 1 ignores the block with a warning; photon method 2 shoots from the emitter.

The block takes no position. The light is placed, transformed and copied with its object. The container must be a finite object. A candle flame, its glow authored to match:

sphere {
  0, 1 pigment { rgbt 1 }
  interior {
    media { mix add density { spherical color_map { [0 rgb 0] [0.4 rgb 0.3] [1 rgb 1] } } }
    light { pigment { rgb <14, 6, 1.6> * 9 } samples 32 }
  }
  scale <0.06, 0.16, 0.06> translate <0, 1.45, 0>
}

3.12.2 Radiance, Attenuation and Cutoff

The light states radiance: what a white diffuse probe reads. A probe immersed in the emitter, or facing an optically thick patch of it, renders exactly the stated radiance, at any scene scale, and no receiver anywhere is lit beyond π times it, apart from the light a medium's own absorption removes on the way. The renderer never converts the number; brightness 5 nits writes 5 to the light wherever the scene is metres or centimetres.

Each point of the volume carries an emission coefficient of L × σ, with L the pigment times brightness and σ the local density-weighted extinction of the interior's media, absorption plus scattering's share, the quantity the media marcher already works with. Where the medium has nothing, no light is generated; where it is dense, the light saturates at the stated radiance rather than growing with the density. A medium without any extinction, a pure glowing gas, falls back to the container's Cauchy mean chord 4V/A: its emission is L over that chord, shaped by the normalized density field, so the gas lights with the same total power as an equal-radiance surface over its silhouette whatever its density map says. Either way the light's colour, the radiant intensity the renderer uses, is the integral of the emission over the volume over π, so a white surface facing a thick patch of the emitter renders the radiance itself, and a ball of gas of radius r lights the ground like a point light of colour L r2 with fade_distance 0 fade_power 2.

lumens states the total power instead of the radiance, under the fixed convention that one scene unit is one metre: the radiance is the lumens over π times the container's surface area, measured on the emitter's grid. In a scene built at c metres to the unit, scale_lumens(Phi, c), a standard function returning Phi / c2, carries a lumens figure from the metre convention into the scene's own units.

Falloff is geometry, not a formula: the light arriving is the solid angle the emitter covers, and the only physical attenuation along the way is the transmittance of the media the shadow ray crosses, which the light's own absorption already provides. attenuation Scale, Power is an artistic shape on top: the applied factor is A′ = A · (1/Scale)1−A, with A = (R/max(r, R))Power, r the distance from the emitter's centre and R the emitter's bounding radius. It is continuous at the pivot r = R, leaves the light untouched at the source, and is exactly 1/Scale times fainter in the far field; a scale of 0 keeps the light's reach from dimming at all. One value gives the scale alone, the power staying 2. fade_power and fade_distance are not of this block: they belong to classic lights, where a point light approximates a larger light of unknown size; for a real volume that justification falls away.

fade_cutoff lets distant points skip a light that would add nothing visible to them. What the light can deliver at a point is bounded by its radiance times the solid angle of its bounding sphere at the point's nearest reach, over the attenuation scale; when that bound falls below fade_cutoff the light is left out at that point: it adds nothing there and spends no shadow ray, cache entry or media sample work. The value is on the same 0 to 1 scale as adc_bailout. Scenes of #version 4.0 and later default to 0.000004, about a thousandth of the default adc_bailout; earlier versions default to 0, which keeps their renders unchanged. #default { interior { light { fade_cutoff Value } } } gives lights parsed after it that starting value instead, in any version, as #default gives default values; a fade_cutoff written in the light overrides either. The render statistics give the number of light tests it left out.

The medium's emission is the glow alone: it paints pixels and lights nothing, as in every release before media lights. A lantern's visible glow comes from its haze scattering the light, or from an authored emission look. To keep a light matching its glow, give them the same colour.

3.12.3 Sampling and Shadows

When the scene is parsed, POV-Ray builds a table of the power of each cell of a grid over the container, counting only points inside it, as the light's far field and its photon shooting need positions drawn by power. The grid is at the media's method 4 resolution, or the width method 4 would choose for the container; a grid over one million cells is not refused: the cell width is raised by 5 per cent at a time until it fits. The grid's resolution is a sampling detail: the light's power is the integral of the law over the volume, and the table converges to it, so changing the resolution changes cost and noise, not illumination. A container that is a deferred mesh (isosurface_mesh, skein_mesh or a .povm mesh) is built then, on the parser thread and one at a time, instead of in parallel with the other meshes.

A lit point closer to the light than far_radii of its radii, the near field, draws samples positions from it: a direction is drawn over the cone the emitter covers and followed along its chord inside the container, the exact estimator of the emitter's whole contribution, with no singularity however close the receiver is, even inside the emitter. Each draw traces one shadow ray. Farther away, the far field, the emitter's centre stands for it: one shadow ray, the same illumination, a fraction of the cost, since almost all of a scene's lit points lie far from their lights. far_radii 0 keeps the volume everywhere.

  • The cost is samples shadow rays per lit point in the near field and one in the far field. There are none where the whole emitter is behind the surface, unless the finish has diffuse backlighting or the object is double_illuminate.
  • With media_attenuation, each shadow ray is attenuated by the media of every interior it passes, including the light's own medium and any smoke surrounding it, so smoke dims its flame.
  • shadowless casts no shadows. media_interaction off leaves scattering media unlit by this light.
  • shadows method 1 walks this light's shadow rays without the per-light shadow cache.
  • A scattering medium is lit by one point of the light per media sample, as by an area light. Its magnitude is the light's escaped power as a point at the emitter's centre: the same as a classic light of that power, exact beyond far_radii, and nearer somewhat gentler under the same bounded law a surface receives. The exact chord integral is a surface's alone; a medium sample has no cosine to bound it, and inside an emitter that integral would grow without bound, so the law lights it. Where a constant or coarser scattering medium lit by a volume light shares a portion with a finer prepared medium, its scattered light is found at its own cadence; see Sampling Parameters & Methods.
  • Shadow rays travel straight, also through media refraction. Emitters inside glass are stated in vacuum units.

cluster_radii serves scenes with many small shadowless lights, a street of lamps or a spiral of sparks. Lights with the same cluster_radii, the same attenuation and the same media switches form a tree in space; a point farther than cluster_radii of a node's radius is lit by that node as one light, nearer ones descend to the leaves. It needs shadowless: a cluster lights a surface without shadow rays. The strata of the volume table have equal power, so the noise of the soft shadows falls as samples rises. The sample scenes use 16 to 32 and take the count as Declare=Samples=N.

3.12.4 Media, Light Groups and Radiosity

  • The glow. The camera, reflections and refractions still see the medium's glow, and gathers of radiosity { media on } see it like any other medium's, since the light is stated apart from the emission.
  • Light groups. An object with a light interior inside a light_group lights the objects of that group only, as the group's light sources do. See Light Groups.
  • Other media. The light follows its own law over the interior's media and ignores the mix and priority of other media: a subtract or replace medium that carves the glow does not carve the light. It is dimmed by other media only through media_attenuation.
  • Several lights. Every interior with a light block is its own light.
  • Copies. A declared object placed several times, scaled or moved, has one light per placement, and a radiance-anchored light renders the same at every scale.

3.12.5 Limits and Messages

  • Only an object's interior can hold a light. Atmospheric media cannot.
  • A light interior's media must add or replace. They cannot mix subtract or mix multiply.
  • A light interior's photons are shot by photon method 2; photon method 1 ignores its photons block with a warning.
  • Volume lights are not yet seen through portals.
A medium's light_source block moved out of media to its interior: interior { media { ... } light { ... } }.Error: the old form, a light_source block inside media.
A light block has no fade_power; its falloff is attenuation <scale>[, <power>].Error: fade_power in a light block. Likewise fade_distance.
An interior light needs media that add or replace, not ones that subtract or multiply.Error: mix subtract or mix multiply in the interior.
A light block's brightness must be finite and not negative.Error.
An interior light gives no light because ...Warning, and the media render but do not light. The reason is one of: its container is infinite; its container is unbounded; its container is empty; its density is negative or not finite; its density is zero throughout its container; its media have no extinction for the light to draw on; its brightness or pigment is zero.
photons in an interior light are ignored by photon method 1.Warning.

3.12.6 Worked Examples

All of these are in scenes/interior/media and take the options named in their first comment lines with Declare=.

3.12.6.1 A Candle in a Misty Room

blend_candle.pov is the flame of 3.12.1 in a room filled with mist. The flame and its smoke add to the mist, so they use mix add; the mist and smoke dim the flame's light on its way to the surfaces, because media_attenuation is on. Declare=FlameLight=0 swaps the media light for a point light at the wick, to compare, and FlameSamples sets the samples.

3.12.6.2 Tubes and Neon

light_tubes.pov lights an underground passage only by fluorescent tubes and neon strips. A tube is a thin transparent cylinder with a glowing medium, and a fine resolution so the grid follows its thin shape; one tube is dying and one is dead, by the strength of the light.

cylinder {
  <0, 0, -Length / 2>, <0, 0, Length / 2>, 0.028
  pigment { rgbt 1 }
  interior { media { mix add method 4 resolution 0.009 } light { pigment { rgb Colour * 180 * Strength } samples Samples } }
  translate P
}

3.12.6.3 Glow Inside Other Media

light_clouds.pov lets sky lanterns drift into a cumulus and light it from inside, and a flash light a storm tower from within. light_explosion.pov shades a fireball's light with its own smoke and warps the street behind it with the heat. light_fish.pov makes glowing fish and jellyfish the only light in water that dims and tints it (Declare=Murk=0 clears the water). In each, the light is stated as radiance in the interior's light block, and the surrounding media act on it through media_attenuation and, for the scattering, media_interaction.

3.12.6.4 Overlapping and Contained Lights

light_intersections.pov overlaps red, green and blue lights, threads them with a bronze knot, holds a glass ball in one and crosses them with smoke; it also seals a glow in a cracked stone and sinks a pool into the floor. light_colonnade.pov lights a rotunda only by a glowing core at its centre, with a lens of space around it made by media refraction (Declare=Refract=0 drops it).
