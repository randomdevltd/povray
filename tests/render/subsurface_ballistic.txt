The unlit 1 mm slab isolates the unscattered contribution. Its unit diffuse
reflectance selects the exact reduced-albedo endpoint 1 in both inversion
tables, making extinction 1/translucency. No incident light, radiosity or
single-scattering samples contribute. The finite white emission patch is
visible through the slab but missed by the reflected camera ray.

Method 2 should return T01*T10*exp(-path/translucency), using unpolarised Fresnel
power transmission at each interface. Both exterior regions have the same
index, so the two radiance index-squared factors cancel. The expected values
are computed from the incident and transmitted cosines independently of the
renderer's Fresnel implementation. Linear 16-bit PPM limits quantization error.

The method 1 arms retain the released unattenuated-interface behavior. The
conserve_energy arms check that generic reflection does not apply the entry
transmission again. Equal-index arms check that adding the boundary factors
leaves index-matched transmission unchanged.

This test does not validate nested dielectrics or different exterior media at
the two interfaces. The existing SSLT ballistic ray construction refracts by
reciprocal relative indices and does not propagate the actual interior stack
through the slab; those cases require separate path-context handling.

The opaque arm makes ballistic extinction negligible. The central ray in the
wedge enters normally and reaches a far face tilted by 30 degrees, so the two
interface cosines differ; it detects reuse of the entry transmission at exit.

Lit=1 replaces the emission backing with a front light. With Length=.01 this
provides a thick diffuse image for exact comparison with an earlier binary;
ballistic attenuation is already below the tracing cutoff.
