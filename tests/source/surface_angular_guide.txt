Continuous surface angular proposal
===================================

For a bounding sphere of radius R, receiver-to-centre distance d and axis a,
let h=R/(2d+R), y=1-a.w. The spherical Cauchy density is

    p(w) = h^2 / (pi * [y + h^2*(2-y)]^2).

Its cumulative probability from the axis to y is

    F(y) = y / [y + h^2*(2-y)],
    F^-1(u) = 2*u*h^2 / [1-u+u*h^2].

Therefore the density integrates to one over solid angle. It is positive on
the entire sphere, uniform at d=0, and continuous through d=R. Computing y as
|a-w|^2/2 avoids cancellation for small angles. Common scaling of d and R
keeps h stable without changing the distribution.

The whole-object component retains half of the mixture probability. Cell
probabilities are proportional to

    P / (d^2+r^2) * [1 + N.(receiver-centre)/sqrt(d^2+r^2)],

where N is the power-weighted mean unit normal. A shared r^2 factor cancels
from these probabilities. Since |N|<=1 and r>0, this heuristic is positive
and continuous. It changes the proposal only; actual outgoing radiance and
its one-sided cosine remain part of the target. Stable sum-of-squares
identities retain the small positive factor for distant back-facing cells.

For x=d/R<=1, the whole-sphere kernel density is at least 1/(36*pi), or
1/9 of the old uniform full-sphere proposal. For x>=1, every direction
intersecting the bound has 1-cos(theta)<=1/x^2. Writing t=1/(x+1) and
g=x/(x+1), the equivalent density is

    t^2*(2-t)^2 / [4*pi*(t^2+2*g*(1-cos(theta)))^2].

Thus its ratio to the old uniform cone density is at least
(2*x+1)^2/[4*(3*x+2)^2]>=9/100. Including the half-weight baseline gives
a lower bound of 9/200 times the old proposal on every contributing
direction. Uniform selection among K forward roots consequently uses K/q,
where q is the complete mixture density, including all overlapping kernels.
Misses contribute zero; they are never retried or conditioned on a hit.

These bounds control proposal weights. They do not promise finite variance
for every permitted grazing radiance singularity. Second-moment continuity
requires a square-integrable target; the tests use bounded radiance.

The source test checks normalization by logarithmic angular quadrature,
inverse CDFs, scales from 1e-12 to 1e12, receiver distances through 1e12 radii,
PDF and second-moment continuity at the whole bound and every occupied cell,
and actual sphere roots with a bright patch. The former cone sampler is
retained only inside the test for variance and cost comparisons. Reported
variance times nanoseconds measures the cheap analytic test; expensive scene
intersection and attenuation costs must be included in renderer comparisons.

Compile surface_angular_guide.cpp with the configured renderer's include paths
and configuration definitions. Run both ordinary optimized flags and its
production floating-point flags. Assertions must remain enabled.

Each tracing thread retains one conditioned distribution keyed by the exact
receiver coordinates and a generation assigned when a guide is constructed
or prepared. Reusing a distribution changes no probabilities. Re-preparation,
a different guide, or any receiver-coordinate change invalidates the entry.
The tests compare cached kernel parameters and probabilities bit for bit,
and bound floating-point differences in sampled directions and densities.
