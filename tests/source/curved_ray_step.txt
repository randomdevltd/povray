The midpoint integrator must stop its differential equation at a surface before
applying the surface's index transition. A full trial may evaluate its midpoint
beyond a prepared field's support. Its zero gradient then omits bending that
belongs to the part of the trial still inside the field. Interpolating the full
trial's direction to the intersection cannot recover that missing bend.

The CPU test retains an independent numerical reproduction for n(x)=1.1+0.1x,
normal incidence at z=0, exit at z=1, and a screen one unit beyond the exit.
For x=-0.00625 and step 0.03, the original 34-step construction gives screen
x=0.13876988889827008 against the exact Fermat-invariant solution
x=0.13990558814819273. The last step starts at z=0.9886671330030111;
its boundary fraction is 0.3793374038500097, putting the full midpoint outside.
The resulting error is -0.001135699249922656, although the midpoint method is
second order on a smooth field. This boundary defect is first order: the
remaining inside arc length is O(step), and its entire direction change is lost.

MidpointCurvedRayStep is the production stage evaluator. At an existing detected
hit, RefineCurvedRayBoundary reintegrates shortened stages and queries their
corrected chords until the intersection and integrated endpoint agree to a
floating-point tolerance. Field support remains unchanged. Ordinary steps make
no additional intersection queries. Refinement permits twelve queries; failure
retries a halved marching step. At the minimum marching step, a local curvature
update supplies the terminal direction without extending the field.

The tests exercise both midpoint phases, several step widths, the exact planar
index invariant, a curved surface with second-order directional convergence,
missing intersections, and bounded nonconvergence. The screen formula checks
the planar exterior Snell transition independently of rendering and brightness.
