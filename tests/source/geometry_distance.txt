Geometry intersection distance policy

Primitive intersection tests accept finite roots above their existing lower
depth tolerance. They do not impose an additional maximum viewing distance.
The caller chooses the segment endpoint: an ordinary nearest-hit query is open,
while a finite tracing segment or shadow connection excludes hits past its end.
Local transformations must not change that policy.

Box slab intervals start at the largest representable depth and are narrowed
by the actual slabs. Isosurface root searches retain the finite interval of
their container, rather than clipping it to an unrelated world distance.
Nearest-hit sentinels in mesh, lathe and surface-of-revolution traversal follow
the same policy. MAX_DISTANCE and BOUND_HUGE retain their other meanings.

The CPU regression calls actual All_Intersections and Find_Intersection paths
at distances 100, 384400000 and 149600000000. The radius is 0.004 times distance.
It checks a direct sphere, a nonuniformly scaled sphere, box, direct and scaled
meshes with bounding hierarchies, and a contained isosurface. The isosurface
function is x*x+y*y+z*z-0.25, with max_gradient 4 and accuracy 1e-7.
Finite endpoints on either side of each entry are checked for both image and
shadow rays. A linear quadric must emit exactly one finite root, so its absent
second root cannot become an artificial surface. Explicit quadric bounding and
clipping objects still reject excluded intersections; an automatically bounded
quadric retains its finite bounds. NaN and infinities are rejected
even when the compiler enables fast floating-point optimizations.

The sphere, box, mesh and isosurface also run through actual legacy and flat
scene trees, with both open queries and finite endpoints on either side of the
entry. The legacy queue starts from the caller's current closest depth and uses
the actual slab interval, preserving the infinite-node queue ordering sentinel.
Forward and inverse bounding-box transformations retain translated coordinates
in both directions, with no clipping to a fixed world-coordinate cube.

Flat and quantized bounding slabs also check zero direction components and
nonzero components of magnitude 1e-32, with hit and miss directions. Parallel
axes test containment directly; risky nonzero axes use double-precision slab
division instead of an overflowing or clamped float reciprocal.

From a configured build of the matching source, run:

  sh tools/bench/check-geometry-distance.sh BUILD_DIRECTORY OUTPUT_DIRECTORY
  sh tools/bench/check-sphere-intersection.sh BUILD_DIRECTORY OUTPUT_DIRECTORY

Remaining limitation: the Sturm polynomial solver still brackets positive roots
with MAX_DISTANCE. Removing primitive acceptance limits does not expand that
solver's search. A separate change must derive a finite coefficient or geometry
bound; an infinite root-search endpoint is unsuitable for its evaluations.
